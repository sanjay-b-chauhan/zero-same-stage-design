### "I don't know" becomes a micro-scenario (owner: games builds the card, stage wires it)
- The second time the learner says they do not know on the same open slot (the engine's `STUCK` regex, or the I'm stuck toggle), she does not explain. One line ("Let's play it out.") and a game card lands in the chat as her message (`msg.game = { id, done }`).
- It is experiential, never a quiz. His words: "it has to be experiential... If something doesn't go well, it's fine. We can help the user... It is like learning by doing... by knowing what problem you are facing." The learner DOES something on the real rows and the moment plays forward from what they did. (Research backs it: an answer-giving AI tutor raised practice 48% and cut exam scores 17%, Bastani et al. PNAS 2025; a hint-only one removed most of the harm.)
- The card: a real moment in two lines (who, when, what is at stake), a live slice of the file (3 to 6 rows) the learner acts on: tap the row or cell they think is wrong, or pick an action ("Send it to finance as is", "Sort by customer_id first", "Ask Eryn"). An action is something you do, never an answer to a question. What happens next plays out as a tiny scene with real numbers: Finance replies "Brazil is up 9% this week?", the total in the slice changes, Eryn says one line. A move that goes badly is part of the story, never a red X: the scene shows the problem, then the learner acts again. When they find it, the slot fills from the card, the XP flies from the card, she names what they found in one line. After two moves that go badly she plays the right move with them, as a colleague, not a reveal. No timers, no lives, no confetti, no score screen, no "Correct" or "Wrong".

## The one design language

The Zero Jobs kit, as a finished, measured system. Read `~/claudecode/zero-jobs-design-system/v1/DESIGN.md` before writing any UI, and look at `~/claudecode/zero-jobs-design-system/v1/starter.html`, `screens/`.
